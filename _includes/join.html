Prospective PhD students

Thanks for your interest in the Praxis Lab! I know applying to grad school is stressful, and I appreciate the time people put into looking at research groups.

If you’d like to do a PhD in my lab, the best path is to apply through the Cornell CS PhD program, the Cornell ME PhD program, or the Cornell Robotics PhD program. Please mention my name and specific research overlap in your application. You don’t need to email me first. I read the applications that come in through admissions, and not emailing won’t hurt your chances.

I get more email from prospective students than I can answer, and I’m sorry I can’t reply to everyone. If you don’t hear back, it says nothing about you or your application. You can read more about my group’s research here.

Cornell undergraduates and master’s students

If you’re a Cornell undergraduate or master’s student interested in research opportunities with our lab, please complete this interest form rather than emailing me directly.

We look at responses as spots open up, and we’ll follow up if there’s a good match.

Current Cornell PhD students

If you’re already a PhD student at Cornell and are looking for an advisor, please feel free to reach out. Please send an email that includes:

  • Subject line: [Potential Advising] your_subject
  • A short description of your background and links to representative work
  • Where you see overlap with my lab’s research

Right now, I can only advise students in the CS and ME fields. If I think there's a good match, I can reach out to set up a time to chat.

FAQ

Should I email you about my PhD application?
You don’t need to, and it won’t affect your application. Due to the volume of inquiries, I usually can’t respond individually before the admissions process. If you’re admitted, please reach out — I’d be happy to find a time to chat.
Which program should I apply to?
I can currently advise students from the CS, ME, and Robotics PhD programs. If you apply through Robotics, make sure to list CS or ME as your field of study (since I’m a member of those two fields).
What background do you look for in undergrad/master's researchers?
Hands-on experience goes a long way — things like project teams, embedded systems, ROS, or mechanical prototyping. Coursework in robotics, control, or machine learning also helps.
How can I get experience before applying to your lab?
Join a student project team, take advanced robotics or controls courses, or build your own small projects. These are the best ways to prepare for research in our group.
Are you recruiting postdocs?
Not currently. I am planning to consider hiring postdocs once the lab is more established.
